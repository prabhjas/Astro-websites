August 3, 2026

For millions of Muslim employees in Saudi Arabia, Hajj is far more than a journey—it is a once-in-a-lifetime religious obligation and one of the Five Pillars of Islam. Yet many private sector employees are unsure whether taking time off for Hajj could affect their salary, annual leave balance, or even their job security.

The good news is that Saudi Labour Law protects eligible employees by granting a dedicated Hajj leave entitlement. Under Article 114, qualifying Muslim employees are entitled to 10 to 15 days of fully paid leave to perform Hajj, with Eid Al-Adha holidays included within this period. In April 2026, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD) also reaffirmed these existing provisions to improve awareness among employers and employees.

In this guide, we’ll explain everything you need to know about Hajj leave in Saudi Arabia, including who qualifies, how many days of leave are granted, whether the leave is paid, how to apply, and what your rights are if an employer refuses a valid request.

What Is Hajj Leave?

Hajj Leave Meaning:

Many workers confuse regarding religious leaves for Hajj and often wonder if they are different from annual leaves or not.

The answer is, both are different from each other!

Hajj leave is a statutory leave entitlement. These leaves are granted for performing the Hajj pilgrimage. It is protected under Saudi Labour Law and exists separately from annual leave and public holidays.

Pilgrimage leaves are fully paid for eligible Muslim employees. The benefit gives them an opportunity to perform Hajj without losing income or employment benefits.

Unlike annual leave, religious leave for Hajj isn’t a company benefit. It is a legal right established under Article 114 of the Saudi Labour Law.

The importance of this leave goes beyond employment regulations. Hajj is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. This entitlement is a reflection of Saudi Arabia’s commitment to supporting religious obligations in the workplace.

What Does Saudi Labour Law Say About Hajj Leave?

Understanding the Hajj leave Saudi labor law provisions starts with Article 114.

Under Article 114, eligible employees are entitled to ten to fifteen days of fully paid leave. The leaves given to them are specifically for performing Hajj. Granted once during an employee’s career. Eid Al-Adha holidays are included within the leave period.

The exact number of days depends on operational requirements and employer discretion within the legal range.

Hajj Pilgrimage Leave Entitlement at a Glance

The entitlement you’ll get under religious leaves is described for your better understanding.

In April 2026, HRSD clarified that this was not a new policy. Instead, it reinforced existing legal protections and encouraged compliance across the private sector.

Eid Al-Adha holidays are counted within the Hajj pilgrimage leave period. They aren’t added separately. Many employees miss this key point. But you shouldn’t!

Employers are allowed to regulate scheduling based on business requirements. Still, they can’t permanently refuse qualified employees. They must provide valid legal reasons against it.

Eligibility Criteria for Hajj Pilgrimage Leave in Saudi Arabia?

The Hajj leave rules are simple to qualify. You must meet three conditions simultaneously as an employee.

First → The employee must be Muslim.

Second → The employee must never have performed Hajj before.

Third → The employee must have completed at least two consecutive years of service with the current employer.

The biggest misconception circulating is that this entitlement only applies to Saudi nationals. In reality, that is incorrect. The Hajj leave in Saudi Arabia is granted to every expatriate worker, and they may receive the same protection as Saudi citizens. Simply put, any Muslim employee who satisfies the legal requirements qualifies.

But make sure you have never been on Hajj before. Because if an employee has already completed Hajj at any point in life, the entitlement no longer applies. It will still apply even if you recently joined a new company.

Is Religious Leave For Hajj Paid? How Is Salary Calculated?

You may be wondering:

“Will my salary be reduced during the Hajj pilgrimage leave?”

The answer is clear “NO”!

Religious leave for Hajj is fully paid without any deduction. It is mentioned clearly in the official Saudi Arabia Hajj leave policy. As a worker, you can receive your normal salary during the approved leave period. No deductions are made from your basic salary. You’ll receive fixed allowances with regular contractual benefits.

The leave is completely separate from annual leave. Your employers can’t deduct statutory Hajj leave from annual leave balances. They can’t even replace your religious leaves for Hajj with annual leaves. During the leave period, your wages have not been reduced.

Unfortunately, some firms have attempted to merge annual leave with statutory Hajj leave. This practice violates Article 114 and exposes employers to compliance risks.

Hajj Leave vs. Annual Leave vs. Public Holidays:

Confusion often occurs because employees see all forms of leave as similar. Legally, they are all very different. So what makes them different from each other? Let’s clarify each of them.

Deducted from Annual Leave No N/A No
  • Annual leave accumulates every year.
  • Public holidays apply to everyone.
  • Statutory Hajj leave exists as a one-time religious entitlement.

Even when Eid Al-Adha falls during Hajj pilgrimage leave, employees are not losing holiday benefits. The law already factors those days into the ten to fifteen days’ entitlement.

Keep your eyes open during the process. If your firm offsets one leave type against another, they risk violating Saudi labour regulations.

How to Apply for Religious Leaves for Hajj?

Applying early reduces stress and avoids unnecessary disputes. A step-by-step guide for employees has been briefly discussed to plan your Hajj journey this year.

Step 1 → Submit a Formal Request

Send a written leave application to HR or your manager well before the Hajj season begins.

Step 2 → Provide Supporting Documents

Employers may request proof that this is your first Hajj. You may be asked to show Nusuk registration confirmation, passport travel history, and a personal declaration.

Step 3 → Employer Review

The company reviews eligibility and workforce planning requirements. Employers may stagger leave schedules if multiple employees request statutory Hajj leave simultaneously.

Step 4: Keep Records

You should save copies of leave requests, HR responses, approval letters, and supporting documents. By doing so, a protection is created if disputes arise later.

As a worker, you escalate concerns through the Qiwa dispute resolution system or contact HRSD directly if your request is denied without a valid reason. Remember always, you are requesting a legal entitlement, not asking for a favor!

You should be like a forward-thinking HR department, and you can claim it whenever you want.

HR and Employer Obligations: What Firms Must Do

Employers play an important role in meeting compliance obligations. Under the Saudi Arabia Hajj leave policy, organizations must grant religious leave for Hajj when all legal conditions are satisfied. Employers can determine leave dates, set the duration between ten and fifteen days, request supporting documentation, and manage annual workforce scheduling.

For larger organizations, using HR leave management software can simplify leave approvals, maintain accurate documentation, and help HR teams manage overlapping Hajj leave requests while staying compliant with Saudi Labour Law. 

On the other hand, employers can’t refuse qualified employees permanently. They can’t deduct leave from annual leave balances and reduce salaries. Even discriminating against expatriate workers comes under the violation.

Why Planning Has Significance?

Saudi Arabia hosts millions of pilgrims annually.

According to official Saudi statistics:

“Hajj attendance exceeded 1.8 million pilgrims in 2025. Strong growth is expected in future seasons.”

Proactive planning prevents staffing shortages and operational disruptions. This works well for industries with large Muslim workforces. You should be like a forward-thinking HR department who begin their pilgrimage workforce planning months in advance. When things are done beforehand, many delays and sudden consequences are already eliminated.

Misconceptions & Questions About Statutory Hajj Leave

Many myths are circulating among employees.

It’s time to clear them up!

Can I take Hajj leave more than once?

You can’t take Hajj leaves twice. The entitlement applies once during your lifetime.

Does the two-year requirement restart after changing jobs?

The two consecutive years must be completed with your current employer.

Are Eid holidays added on top?

Eid Al-Adha leaves are included within the ten to fifteen-day leave period.

Can annual leave replace Hajj leave?

These leaves are separate statutory entitlements.

Is Hajj leave only for Saudis?

The Hajj leave applies equally to expatriate Muslim employees.

What if I performed Hajj years ago?

The entitlement no longer applies. The law covers first-time Hajj only.

What Happens If Your Employer Denies Hajj Leave?

Don’t worry! You have all legal options available if you meet every eligibility requirement, and still your employer refuses without a valid justification. We’re going to discuss what steps you can take to justify your case.

Step 1 → Gather Evidence

Keep copies of leave requests, HR communications, service records, and proof of first-time pilgrimage status.

Step 2 → File Through Qiwa

Submit a labour dispute through the Qiwa platform.

Step 3 → Contact HRSD

Report violations directly to the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development. The Saudi labor law Hajj leave provisions are legally enforceable. HRSD’s 2026 clarification strengthened awareness and reduced employer misunderstandings about the law.

The best protection comes from properly documenting everything from the start of the process.

Final Thoughts

Hajj leave in Saudi Arabia is not a company benefit. It is a protected legal right that is granted to every qualified worker. Eligible Muslim employees who have completed two consecutive years with their employer and have never performed a pilgrimage before are entitled to ten to fifteen fully paid days of leave. Their salary remains protected, and annual leave remains untouched. Their rights are backed by the Saudi Labour Law and reinforced by HRSD’s 2026 clarification.

Compliance goes beyond avoiding penalties for employers. Supporting employees during a once-in-a-lifetime religious journey builds trust and loyalty. A chance to create a stronger workplace culture.

Planning to go on Hajj this year? The key is to plan early and document everything. Don’t forget to follow the law. That approach benefits everyone, so it will benefit you, too!

FAQs

What are Hajj leaves in Saudi Arabia?

Under Article 114 of the Saudi Labour Law, Hajj leave is a fully paid statutory leave entitlement. It grants eligible Muslim employees ten to fifteen days off to perform the pilgrimage. These days are separate from annual leave.

How many days of Hajj leave are employees entitled to?

Eligible employees receive between ten and fifteen fully paid days. Eid Al-Adha holidays are included in it. Employers determine the exact duration within this range.

Who qualifies for Hajj leave in Saudi Arabia?

Any Muslim employee, Saudi or expatriate, who has completed two consecutive years with the current employer and has never performed Hajj before qualifies.

Is the Hajj leave deducted from the annual leave?

Pilgrimage leave is a separate statutory entitlement. The leaves granted for Hajj cannot be deducted from annual leave balances.

Can an employer refuse Hajj leave?

An employer can’t refuse an eligible employee without a valid reason. Employees can raise disputes through Qiwa or contact HRSD.

Does the Hajj leave entitlement apply to expatriate employees?

The Saudi Labour Law applies equally to Saudi nationals and expatriate Muslim employees. You only have to meet the eligibility requirements.

What is the legal basis for Hajj leave in Saudi Arabia?

Religious leave for Hajj is governed by Article 114 of the Saudi Labour Law. It was publicly reinforced by HRSD in April 2026.
